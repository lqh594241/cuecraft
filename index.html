FROM ROUGH CUT TO FINAL CUES
A simple workflow. A better watch.
01 / OPEN YOUR CUES
Keep the words. Check the format.
Use UTF-8 SRT or WebVTT. Cues need a start timestamp, an end timestamp and text. NOTE, STYLE and REGION blocks are not carried into the export.
02 / ADJUST THE TIMELINE
A little early. A little late.
A positive shift delays captions; a negative shift brings them forward. Optional FPS correction rescales duration before the shift. Inspect synchronization at both the start and end.
03 / REVIEW BEFORE SHARING
Overlaps deserve a closer look.
The overlap checker identifies simultaneous cues. Optional repair shortens a cue to the next cue’s start; identical start times cannot be repaired automatically.
THE DETAILS BEHIND THE TIMELINE
Good cues.
Clear answers.
A little clarity before the final export.
What does a positive shift do?
A +2 second shift moves every subtitle 2 seconds later. A -2 second shift moves it earlier. If a cue falls before zero, choose trimming or correct the shift.
Does FPS conversion always fix sync?
No. The ratio is useful when captions were timed to a different-duration frame sequence. Videos can change frame rate without changing duration, in which case captions should not be scaled.
Are styles and VTT positioning preserved?
Text payload is kept, and basic WebVTT cue settings can remain in VTT output. NOTE, STYLE and REGION blocks are omitted. SRT output drops VTT identifiers and positioning; check appearance in your player.
Can overlap repair change meaning?
Yes. Shortening a cue reduces how long text remains visible. Automatic repair does not judge readability, multiple speakers or intended overlap. Always review the output.
